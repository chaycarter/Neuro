## Voice & Style Rules (STRICT — never break these)

1. **No em dashes** — ever. Use commas, full stops, or restructure the sentence.
1. **No bold body text** — headers only. Never bold phrases mid-paragraph.
1. **No AI-sounding language** — avoid: "delve", "it's worth noting", "in the realm of", "leverage" (as a verb), "cutting-edge", "game-changer", "transformative", "exciting", "thrilled", "passionate about", "robust", "seamless", "at the end of the day", "in today's fast-paced world".
1. **Tone**: Casual but punchy. Professional without being corporate. Conversational. Direct. Confident without being arrogant.
1. **British/English spelling** preferred (e.g., "specialise" not "specialize").
1. **Short sentences** — preferred over long complex ones.
1. **First person** for all Chay-authored content.

## Content Formats

### LinkedIn Posts (Carter Sciences personal feed)

**Weekly wrap-up post** — summary of activity across both businesses:

- Short. Punchy. Max 150-200 words.
- Bullet-style or short paragraphs — no headers.
- Ends with a CTA or reflective closing line.
- Covers: placements made, articles published, Reccy updates, events/calls, notable moves in the market.
- No hashtag spam — 3-5 max, relevant.

**Reccy Neuro role-specific posts** — highlight a specific neurotech role/opening:

- Lead with market context (why this role matters now).
- Reference the company's position in the neurotech landscape.
- 2-3 short paragraphs max.
- End with link/CTA to Reccy.
- Position Reccy as intelligence platform, not just job board.

### Carter Sciences Interview Articles

**Format**:

- Prose intro paragraph (2-4 sentences — set the scene, who the subject is, why they matter).
- Attribution line: *Interview by Chay Carter*
- **Bold question headers** (these are the only bold text allowed).
- First-person answers in subject's voice.
- Pull quotes (italicised, standalone, 1-2 per interview — the most quotable line).
- No subheadings other than bold questions.
- 1,000-1,800 words typical length.

**Subject framing**: Always establish the subject's credibility and relevance to neurotech in the intro. Don't start with "I'm delighted to…" — just get into it.

Example intro style: "Rebecca Crane has spent the better part of two decades making sure neurotech companies don't ship broken products. As founder of Crane Medtech Partners, she's now the person founders call when they're not sure if their quality systems will survive a 510(k) — or an FDA inspection."

### Substack Articles (theneurotechnewsletter.substack.com)

- Weekly, broad neurotech industry topics.
- Chay as subject matter expert — not just reporting, but offering informed perspective.
- 600-1,200 words.
- Conversational but credible. Like a smart industry insider writing to peers.
- Typically covers: funding news, technology trends, hiring/talent signals, company moves.
- No listicle format — proper flowing prose.

### Newsletter (separate from Substack)

- Bi-weekly, ~1,000 subscribers.
- More detailed than Substack articles.
- Can include data/market intel from Reccy Neuro.

## What to avoid in ALL content

- Overly formal sign-offs ("I hope this finds you well", "Please don't hesitate")
- Passive voice where active is possible
- Vague superlatives ("incredibly", "truly", "really")
- Long rambling intros — get to the point fast
- Claiming things are "the first" or "world's leading" without evidence
